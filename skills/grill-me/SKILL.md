name: grill-me
description: Use when the user says "grill me", or another skill asks for a grill.
---

Settle with the user the decisions about a request, plan or idea that only they can make, and nothing else.

## What's theirs

A decision is theirs when its plausible answers would materially change what they get: what they see it do, compatibility, scope, a tradeoff they'd accept, or the cost to ship. It's settled when the request, a recorded decision, or the user leaving it to you settles it; being asked for a grill leaves nothing to you. Find facts yourself, by looking or trying. Current behaviour shows what exists, and settles what's wanted only where the request or a recorded decision keeps it. How it's built is yours unless the user puts the mechanism in question; when a choice of how brings such a tradeoff, the decision is its consequence, not the mechanism.

Ask an unsettled decision when the request or what you found opens a real fork, or when it defines the outcome or policy the request is about; a confident recommendation doesn't make it yours. A fork is real only when each answer gives the user something the other takes away; when one answer is better for them in every way they'd notice, it's no choice: assume it, and leave what it costs to build to the approach. State the rest as assumptions: the baseline you'll work from, such as keeping what exists, limited to what bounds this request's result, scope or compatibility.

## Asking

Ask in rounds, each as one plain chat message; never use your question tool. A round holds every open question that doesn't wait on another's answer, numbered, each with your recommended answer and worded so "yes" accepts it; a bare "yes" accepts the whole round. Word each question as what the user would see or get under each answer, naming no mechanism and leaving out any part that has no tradeoff. After the questions, list the assumptions not yet stated. A question or assumption that depends on a question still open waits for a later round. Wait for the answers before the next round. An objection to an assumption answers it, or becomes a question when it leaves something open. There's no cap on questions.

## Done

Done when no question is open. Reply with the user's answers, the decisions they left to you, and the assumptions you're working from, each marked as which it is; an assumption is not an answer. With only assumptions left to state, state them and finish without waiting; with nothing to state either, say so in one line. When the user invoked this directly, that reply ends your turn and approves nothing; when another skill called it, that skill says what comes next.

# Craft floor

The quality floor for every screen, in any direction. Each rule is a check you run against the rendered captures, not a style preference. A direction's look is free; these rules are not. A project adds its own product rules (vocabulary, numbers) in its product brief.

## Hierarchy

- **Answer first.** On Operate and Read surfaces, the leading question (product brief) is answered in the first viewport at every target width. The first result is visible without scrolling past headings, explanations or setup. Teaching and long explanations sit behind a disclosure next to what they explain. On Persuade surfaces, the first viewport says what this is and who it is for.
- **Evidence beside the answer.** The facts needed to compare options stay visible together. Never hide comparison data to make a screen look calmer, and never drift toward grey. Colour stays where it carries meaning (category, rating, state, selection).
- **One signal per meaning.** A rating is shown once: a number with its label, plus colour if the direction uses it. Not also a glow, a border, a pulse and the label repeated on every secondary number.
- **Context is visible.** A screen showing a computed result shows, or links in one step, the inputs and settings it was computed for.

## Truthful numbers

- Zero is neutral, never in gain colour or display size. Missing, not applicable and still loading are three states with three different words.
- Every delta names its baseline and says whether it is already included.
- One precision per quantity across screens. A number always carries its unit or basis.
- Counts reconcile with what the list shows.
- Use tabular figures wherever numbers are compared in columns or rows.

## Consistency

- **One selection vocabulary.** Equivalent controls (every switcher, tab set and filter chip) share one selected, hover, focus and disabled treatment, from one component.
- **One name per thing.** A destination's navigation label is its page title. An action keeps one verb through its flow. A state is said once per screen.
- **One icon family** for navigation and actions. Product imagery is used for product objects. No emoji or stray unicode glyphs as UI icons.
- **Labels match content.** Tab and section names match what they hold. A panel with nothing to show is not rendered, or says why it is empty.

## Nothing over the work

- Utility entry points (help, feedback, chat, community) live in the navigation, a menu or a badge. Nothing fixed or floating covers content or controls at any width. When something must be fixed, reserve its space.
- Decoration never crosses reading or data regions. Atmosphere lives in the ground and the margins.
- Primary navigation fits at 1440×900, with no destination clipped or hidden behind an internal scroll.

## Motion

Judge each motion by how often the user sees it.
- Frequent actions respond immediately.
- Motion answers a user action (open, expand, confirm, switch) and shows what changed.
- Nothing loops ambiently behind an Operate surface, and data doesn't animate in on every mount.
- Every animated state has a correct end state with reduced motion on.

## Phone

- Compose the narrow width for the task, not as a squeezed desktop: results within the first screen, controls consolidated into one row or one sheet.
- Tab sets fit without clipping a label.
- Touch targets are at least 44px, and nothing important depends on hover.
- Judge the phone alongside desktop, never after.

## Structure and copy

- **Containers express relationships.** Nest a surface only when the level means something. No decorative box in a box in a box.
- **Template chrome needs a reason a user would name.** This covers an eyebrow above every heading, middot-joined meta strings, one accent-coloured word in a headline, a big-number hero stats row, tracked all-caps micro-labels and "→" appended to links. None is banned, but on Operate surfaces the default is to leave it out, and each kept instance must carry information.
- **Readable meaning.** Text that carries meaning (caveats, labels on numbers) is at body-readable size and at least 4.5:1 contrast, next to what it qualifies, never faint fine print.
- **Plain words.** Sentence case, the user's own vocabulary, and actions named by what happens. The brief's copy and trust boundaries are not negotiable.
- **Stress the data.** Check every screen with the empty, single, full and longest-content fixtures, a no-results search and a failure state before calling it done.
